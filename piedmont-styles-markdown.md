## Purpose of Brand Guidelines

### Using this Document
If you're reading this you probably have an important roll to play in how our brand gets perceived internally, externally or perhaps both. If so, these guidelines were crafted especially for you. The purpose of our brand guidelines is to provide an overview of what the brand stands for and to establish standards for how our brand is seen. When you consider the size, breadth and variety of our organization and the volume of partners we deal with on a daily basis, it is important to have a single source of information that should lead to visual consistency and cohesion across all touchpoints. These guidelines provide a visual* toolbox that include specifications, direction and usage instructions for the logo, colors, typography, photography style, illustration style, and any other essential elements that make up our communication elements. These guidelines will help to build and protect our brand's identity and ensure that we gain recognition, awareness, and loyalty across the state of Georgia and beyond.

*While this style guide sets out clear and deliberate rules and examples of application to cover a wide variety of typical communication elements, and should be a first resource when considering how to apply a Piedmont brand element, it is not the only resource. When in doubt, contact the Brand Growth department for suggestions and approvals for all brand elements.

### Approvals
To ensure the integrity of the brand stays intact, it is critical that all materials are approved by trained brand management personnel. All materials - traditional advertising, collateral, video and digital - that carry a Piedmont logo must go through the proper approval channels. Basic approval processes and best practices are as follows:
* If it includes any Piedmont logo, plan for a Brand Growth department review. This includes advertising and promotional elements, internal communications, common stationery elements such as business cards and all signage, etc.
* Plan for review in schedules - it's important to incorporate reviews and approvals from the Brand Growth department in any timelines, especially for outward-facing elements that have hard deadlines such as magazine ads and event invitations.
* Don't assume compliance - if there's any question as to how a brand element should be used and it is not specifically addressed in the style guide, be sure to get a second opinion from someone in the Brand Growth department.
* Don't be afraid to ask the goal of the style guide and the approval process is not to stifle creativity, but rather to ensure consistent application of brand elements across all channels. The Brand Growth department is committed to working to maximize every effort to promote the Piedmont brand, so if you have an out-of-the-ordinary request, remember that the Brand Growth department is your best resource.

Have a question about the approval process? Email ourbrand@piedmont.org.

## Brand History & Timeline
In 1905, Ludwig Amster, M.D., and Floyd McRae, a surgeon from Georgia's Telfair County, founded Piedmont Hospital, then known as the Piedmont Sanatorium, in a fifteen-room home at the corner of Capitol Avenue and Crumley Street in downtown Atlanta. The two doctors were forward thinkers, and Piedmont Hospital has been a pioneer in patient care since the beginning. Starting in Atlanta and growing over the decades to fulfill the health needs of our neighbors in surrounding communities, Piedmont Healthcare has led the way in bringing world-class health care to the state.

## Verbal Identity
What we say and how we say it is grounded in Piedmont's brand strategy.
* **What is verbal identity?** A purposeful way of communicating who we are that brings our brand and big idea to life, in a way that connects deeply with the people we serve.
* **Messaging is what we say.** Messages are the key ideas behind our communications, both inside and out. They enable our narrative to be consistent, relevant and differentiated. Our messaging should guide our creative expression across touchpoints, offerings, and audiences beyond.
* **Tone of voice is how we say it.** Our tone captures how we want to be heard. Used right, tone of voice helps tell our story in an engaging but also authentic way. We should use our voice across all communications touchpoints and audiences.

### Voice and Tone
Each line of copy should be clear, friendly and uplifting. We want our messaging to reflect that the people of Georgia are at the heart of everything we do. We want to be personable, and we want people to feel confident in our hands, comforted by our human touch, and relieved to get great care each step of the way.

We strive to make the benefits of any tool, service or feature easy to understand and presented in a way that illustrates those benefits. We do so by using words like "we" and "our" to refer to the entities of Piedmont. We do not refer to Piedmont as a separate entity, or as an institution, as if we were not part of it.

**Keywords to consider:** Simplified, Personalized, Empowering, Easier, Better, Seamless, Comprehensive.

### Voice Principles
* **Be a caring and charming host:** Our kind warmth and friendly charm makes you feel right at home, part of a community, and at ease knowing that you're in good hands.
    * **Do:** Speak directly to one individual, using the singular "you." Sound relatable. Use "we" statements to build a connection. Use straightforward, everyday language.
    * **Don't:** Be too casual or flippant. Be unnatural or overly-enthusiastic. Dominate or bore with wordiness or unnecessary information.
* **Lead with confidence:** Exude leadership and conviction in current and future care, inspiring fearlessness in our patients, providers, and stakeholders alike.
    * **Do:** Keep it relevant. Identify the key takeaway and most important details. Share them in clear, memorable soundbites so the reader feels informed and included. Connect to the audience by reflecting their needs in our solutions. Speak in the present tense with an active voice.
    * **Don't:** Overdo it with rhyme and rhythm. When speaking conversationally, a little goes a long way. Avoid using colloquialisms often or outside of headlines, callouts, advertisements, and environments.
* **Be a passionate advocate:** People are the reason we do what we do. Devoted to our community, we are proactive guides driven by empathy and expertise.
    * **Do:** Use warm and welcoming language to keep it human. Use tangible examples that prove our expertise. Accomplishments are best served as show, don't-tell. Invite people along for the journey. Don't just say what we're researching- explain how and why we're making it possible.
    * **Don't:** Lose the story in the tactic. Forget the point of the communication by focusing too much on how the tone feels.

## Logo Guidelines
Our logo is the heart of our visual system. It's approachable, rounded type and interlocking mark personify Piedmont's support at every step of a patient's journey.

* **Minimum logo clear space:** The logo should always have a minimum clear space, free of graphics and text. The required minimum clear space is equal to the "x-height," which is the height of the lowercase letters of the word "Piedmont." The preferred minimum clear space is equal to 1.5x.
* **Full Color Logo:** This is the preferred use of the logo on a neutral-colored background. Use the version appropriate to ensure strong contrast and legibility.
* **Single Color Logo:** These versions should be used only when one-color production is unavoidable, or when placing on a non-neutral solid color.
* **Minimum size:** Always scale the logo proportionally. Logos with a modifier (e.g. Healthcare) should never be smaller than 1 inch in length in print or on-screen.

### Tagline Guidelines & Usage
Piedmont's tagline should be used in all applications where possible. Ratio of tagline to logo should not vary and match the following: full color application or reversed on peach background application.

### Incorrect Logo Usage
The Piedmont logo will become the most recognizable visual aspect of the brand. It's very important to maintain the integrity of the logo in order to keep consistency across all platforms. There are different ways we can use our logo, but it is critical that we use only the approved artwork outlined in this document. Below you'll see some ways NOT to use the logo:
* Change the font or attempt to recreate any part of the logo.
* Place over a complicated photo or background where legibility is compromised.
* Add effects that change the appearance of the logo: e.g., emboss, bevel, drop shadow.
* Stretch, compress or skew the logo.
* Change the color or use gradients in any part of the logo.
* Alter the size, proportions or position of the mark or the type.
* Change the modifier or create another logo with the inclusion of a department or location.
* Use mark or logo type separately or put anything inside the mark.

Adhering to our brand colors ensures that Piedmont communications are consistent and distinctive. A white background should be the foundation for all designs. ## Piedmont Clinic Seal
The Piedmont Clinic "seal" exists to identify physicians, practices or service offerings that are proud members of the program. No other Piedmont logo is used in conjunction with this seal. To ensure correct use, it is important to follow these guidelines:
* **Clear Space:** Piedmont clinic seal and the member logo should never be locked up. The required minimum clear space is equal to 2 times the height of the Piedmont Clinic icon. The seal should always have a minimum clear space, free of graphics and text.
* **Piedmont Clinic Seal:** Do not recreate or alter the Piedmont Clinic seal. Always use artwork as provided.
* **Sizing and Proportion:** Piedmont clinic seal should appear to be optically 30% the size of the member logo. Always scale the seal proportionally. The seal should never be smaller than 0.65 inch in length in print or on-screen.
* **Placement:** Piedmont clinic seal should always be positioned below or in an inferior position to the member logo.

## Typeface Guidelines
Please note the specific use of each typeface and chose the appropriate weight to maximize legibility.
* **Graphik:** Uses: Headlines, subheads and body copy. Weights: Light, Regular, Semibold.
* **Rogthem:** Uses: Tagline, headline phrase and word emphasis. Weight: Regular.
* **Neo Sans STD:** Uses: Logo, type treatments and signage. Weights: Light, Regular.
* **Roboto:** Uses: Website and emails. Weights: Medium, Regular, Bold.

### Typeface Usage
* Tagline typeface is always Rogthem.
* Headlines also utilize Rogthem, although not always for the entire headline. Sometimes, only certain words in the headline will be in Rogthem while the other words are in Graphik Light, depending on what looks best.
* Body copy is always in Graphik Regular and may sporadically incorporate Semibold or Italic Regular for points of emphasis.
* Both headline and body copy type are knocked out white on peach background.

## Visual Guidelines
* **Background:** The background is comprised of multiple masked images, type and gradient layers that need to be individually sized, altered, treated, masked or moved around. Subtle shadows may need to be added in some areas. Backgrounds should bleed off the page or if used in a header, feathered into the white space. In email use, all edges should be hard.
* **Illustrations:** Sketches and splatter must always be in white and reversed out of the brand color background or hero shot. They can be behind, on top of or interacting with photography. Ideally, use 1-3 illustrations where and how they fit, but never force them. Sketches should be relevant to Georgia or convey a general concept, avoid hyper-specific healthcare imagery.
* **Imagery:** Imagery should reflect a diverse representation of Georgians. It should feel natural, not posed, and warm with a mix of looking and not looking at the camera. We want to highlight different lifestyles and industries (family, teacher, tech, etc.), as well as people with devices (smartphone, tablet, computer). There are two ways to encorporate a photo into the layout. Image background can be blended into the brand background. Alternatively, if the background is too simple, the subject can be sillhouetted. In both cases, design elements can be incorporated behind and in front of the subject.

## Text Formatting Guidelines
* **Header/Cover Copy:** Graphik Light, white, sentence case with Rogthem for phrase / word emphasis can be on either side of the visuals but should be vertically aligned within container shape.
* **Headline/Title:** Graphik Light, PMS 173 - larger than body copy.
* **Subheads:** Graphik Semibold, black - between 10 and 12 point type depending on amount of content.
* **Addresses:** Spell out all items (avenue, road, suite, Georiga) when space allows.
* **Phone Numbers:** Use periods instead of dashes, no parenthesis for the area code.
* **Times:** 12-hour format, use number only for on the hour times, use the word to for blocks of time, use lowercase with periods for morning/afternoon (e.g. 8 a.m. to 4:30 p.m.).
* **URLs:** Shorter the better, do not use www (e.g. piedmont.org) - do not use a period after when it's at the end of a call to action.
